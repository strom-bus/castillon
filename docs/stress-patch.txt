Load test for the audio engine.

Forty-eight oscillators as siblings under one Ignite, covering all ten waveforms, all four filter
types and all four sequence lengths, plus five reverbs and five other effects, at 300 BPM on loop.
Paste the code below into the PATCH CODE field.

Siblings rather than a deep cascade, which is the whole difference from the version this replaces.
A cascade sounds one level at a time, so twenty-four oscillators spread down five levels were a
handful playing at any instant — it measured 22 points out of 2750, which is one per cent, and had
been sitting there unnoticed since the ceiling was believed to be a hundred.

The load, by the model's own arithmetic:

  voices     1870   48 oscillators, each holding about 19 at a time
  effects     742   five reverbs at their longest, and five cheaper ones
  total      2611   against a layering threshold of 2063 and a ceiling of 2750

So it is meant to sit past the point where the engine starts degrading and short of where it
breaks. That is the interesting place: a load test that always glitches tells you nothing, and one
that never does tells you less.

What to listen and look for:
  - crackling or dropouts, which would mean the audio thread is missing its window
  - the output clipping rather than being held by the limiter
  - the load meter going bright, and retriggered oscillators restarting rather than layering. That
    is the designed degradation, not a fault.

One thing the meter will not tell you: it reads about half of what this costs. The estimate caps
how many voices an oscillator is assumed to be holding at four, and these hold nineteen — a cap
that keeps the dice honest and makes it blind to exactly this. Trust your ears over the number
here.

The code is checked on every test run (see src/state/stressPatch.test.ts), so a change to the
patch-code format cannot rot it silently — CI fails instead.
